Korean-Inspired Ground Beef Bowl: A Simple Skillet Recipe

Make a savory-sweet ground beef bowl with garlic, ginger, rice and crisp cucumber, plus ways to adjust the sauce and assemble the toppings.

This approachable bowl combines ground beef with a garlic-ginger sauce, cooked rice and cool cucumber. It is a Korean-inspired home-cooking idea, not a claim to a traditional bulgogi recipe. Ingredients for four modest bowls

For the skillet: 1 pound (450 g) ground beef, 1 teaspoon neutral cooking oil if needed, 3 minced garlic cloves and 2 teaspoons finely grated fresh ginger. For the sauce: 3 tablespoons reduced-sodium soy sauce, 1 tablespoon brown sugar, 1 tablespoon rice vinegar, 2 tablespoons water and 1 teaspoon toasted sesame oil.

For serving: 3 cups cooked rice, 1 thinly sliced cucumber and 2 sliced scallions. Optional additions are toasted sesame seeds and a small amount of chili flakes. Soy, wheat and sesame may be present depending on the products you choose; check labels for your dietary needs. Start rice preparation first if it is not already cooked.

Cook the beef and sauce

1. Mix the soy sauce, sugar, vinegar, water and sesame oil in a small bowl. Stir until the sugar is mostly dissolved. Prepare the cucumber and scallions on a clean board before handling raw beef.

2. Heat a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the neutral oil only if the pan needs it, then add the beef. Break it into small crumbles with a spatula and cook, stirring as needed. Drain excess fat carefully if it pools.

3. Reduce the heat to medium. Add the garlic and ginger and stir briefly until fragrant, without letting the garlic scorch. Pour in the sauce and stir so it coats the crumbles. Add a spoonful of water if the pan becomes dry before the meat is ready.

4. Cook ground beef to an internal temperature of 160°F (71°C), checked with a food thermometer. Color alone does not establish doneness. Continue cooking if the measurement is below that temperature. Once safely cooked, taste the sauce and adjust with a little water or vinegar rather than automatically adding more soy sauce.

Assemble with contrast

Divide the cooked rice among four bowls. Add the hot beef, then the cucumber and scallions. Keep the cucumber separate from the skillet so it stays crisp. Finish with optional sesame seeds or chili flakes and serve promptly.

For a more vegetable-forward bowl, replace some rice with cooked vegetables. For less sweetness, begin with half the sugar and adjust the sauce after the beef is fully cooked. Different soy sauces vary in saltiness, so use a measured starting amount rather than pouring directly from the bottle.

Plan leftovers carefully

Keep raw meat utensils away from the prepared toppings and cooked food. Wash hands and clean surfaces after handling raw beef. Refrigerate leftovers promptly in shallow containers and keep the cucumber separately for better texture.

When repeating the recipe, record the sauce balance and rice portion you preferred. Cooking time varies with the skillet, heat and size of the crumbles, so use the temperature check as the safety decision rather than relying on a promised number of minutes.

Frequently asked questions

Is this traditional Korean bulgogi?

No. It is a Korean-inspired ground beef bowl with a simplified garlic-ginger sauce.

Can I replace the soy sauce?

Use a substitute appropriate for your needs and check its label. Its salt level and flavor may require adjusting the water and sugar.

Is browned beef always done?

No. Check that ground beef reaches 160°F (71°C) with a food thermometer.
