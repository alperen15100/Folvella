Banana Syrup for Coffee: A Small-Batch Recipe and 5 Drink Ideas

Make banana syrup for coffee with a small-batch recipe, a simple iced latte method and five hot, cold and decaf drink ideas.

By Trendora Editorial · Updated 2026-10-04 · 3 min read

Banana coffee works best when the fruit becomes a gentle accent rather than a thick purée. This small-batch syrup separates the flavoring step from the drink, so you can adjust sweetness one spoonful at a time.

The recipe below is an original home-kitchen starting point, not a laboratory-tested formulation. It includes one base latte and five serving directions, with AI-generated imagery used for visual inspiration.

Small-batch banana syrup ingredients

For one small jar, use 1 medium ripe banana, peeled and sliced; 100 g granulated sugar (about ½ US cup); 120 ml water (½ US cup); and ¼ teaspoon vanilla extract, optional. Choose a fragrant banana with brown speckles rather than a green one. A very overripe banana may make the finished syrup cloudier.

You will also need a small saucepan, fine-mesh strainer and clean heat-safe container. This is a fruit-infused syrup, not banana purée: keeping the pulp out makes it easier to mix into coffee. Yield varies with simmering and straining; expect roughly 150–200 ml rather than an exact bottle size.

How to make the syrup

Combine sugar and water in the saucepan over medium-low heat. Stir until the sugar dissolves, then add the banana. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook for about 8–10 minutes, stirring occasionally. Keep the heat low enough to prevent rapid evaporation or caramelization; the aim is a softly infused syrup.

Remove from the heat and let the banana steep for 10 minutes. Strain without aggressively pressing the fruit, then add the optional vanilla. Let the syrup cool before adding it to iced drinks. If it becomes thicker than you want, stir in a little hot water, a teaspoon at a time.

Build a banana iced latte

For one drink, combine 1 tablespoon cooled syrup with 30–60 ml espresso or 60 ml strongly brewed coffee. Fill a glass with ice, add 180 ml milk, and pour in the coffee mixture. Stir before drinking and adjust with an extra teaspoon of syrup only if needed.

Choose a medium roast with caramel or nutty notes if you want the banana to remain noticeable. Unsweetened oat milk gives a rounded result; dairy milk makes a familiar café-style latte. The layered look in the illustration is a serving idea, and the drink will naturally blend once stirred.

Five ways to use your banana coffee syrup

1. Hot banana latte: dissolve the syrup in espresso, then add warm milk. 2. Banana cold brew: use 1 tablespoon syrup in 180 ml cold brew and add milk to taste. 3. Cinnamon banana latte: finish the base latte with a light pinch of cinnamon.

4. Banana mocha: dissolve 1 teaspoon cocoa in the hot coffee before adding the syrup and milk. 5. Decaf banana coffee: make the same drink using decaf espresso. Keep each addition modest so the banana does not disappear beneath several competing flavors.

Troubleshooting sweetness and texture

A drink that tastes flat may need stronger coffee rather than more syrup. If the banana tastes too faint, steep the fruit a little longer next time instead of boiling hard. If the syrup is cloudy, use a finer strainer; do not expect a fresh-fruit infusion to behave exactly like a commercial clear syrup.

Make a modest batch for your next few drinks. Transfer the cooled syrup to a clean container and refrigerate promptly; this recipe is not a shelf-stable preserve. Use clean utensils and discard it if you notice mold, fermentation or an unusual smell. No tested shelf-life claim is made for this original recipe.

Frequently asked questions

Can I blend banana into coffee instead?

Yes, but that makes a thicker blended drink with fruit pulp. The strained syrup method is intended for a smoother latte.

Can I use a green banana?

A ripe fragrant banana gives a more obvious banana flavor. An underripe banana is less useful for this infusion.

Does it need an espresso machine?

No. Use strongly brewed coffee or decaf and adjust the milk to your preferred strength.
