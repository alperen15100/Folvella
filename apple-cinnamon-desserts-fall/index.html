8 Apple Cinnamon Desserts for Cozy Fall Evenings

Make eight small-batch apple cinnamon desserts, including crumble cups, puff pastry roses and baked apples, with quantities and visual doneness checks.

Apple desserts become easier when you choose the texture first. Small diced pieces soften quickly in a crumble, thin slices bend into pastry roses, and halved apples hold a filling. Taste your apples before adding sugar: a sweet variety needs less than a sharp one.

These original recipe starting points have not been presented as kitchen-tested recipes. Timing varies with the apple variety, dish size and oven. Check tenderness with a knife and look for cooked pastry or a golden topping rather than relying only on the clock.

1. Individual apple crumble cups

For 4 oven-safe ramekins, toss 3 peeled diced apples with 1 tablespoon brown sugar, 1 teaspoon cinnamon and 1 teaspoon lemon juice. Rub 40 g cold butter into 50 g flour, 40 g rolled oats and 30 g brown sugar. Divide fruit and topping between dishes; bake at 180°C / 350°F for about 25–35 minutes until bubbling and tender.

2. Puff pastry apple roses

For 6 roses, thinly slice 2 cored red apples and soften the slices briefly in hot water with a little lemon juice; drain and dry. Cut about 250 g ready-rolled puff pastry into 6 strips, brush with 2 tablespoons warmed apricot jam mixed with ½ teaspoon cinnamon, overlap apple slices along one edge, fold and roll. Bake in a greased muffin tin at 190°C / 375°F for about 35–45 minutes until the pastry is cooked through; loosely cover if the apple edges darken early.

3. Oat-filled baked apple halves

Halve and core 2 large apples. Mix 30 g oats, 20 g chopped walnuts, 20 g softened butter, 1 tablespoon maple syrup and ½ teaspoon cinnamon. Divide into the cavities and place in a small baking dish with a splash of water. Bake at 180°C / 350°F for about 30–40 minutes until a knife enters the flesh easily.

4. Quick skillet cinnamon apples

Cook 2 sliced apples with 15 g butter over medium heat for about 5 minutes, stirring. Add 1 tablespoon brown sugar, ½ teaspoon cinnamon and 2 tablespoons water. Cover briefly, then uncover and cook until tender with a light syrup. Serve warm over ice cream or yogurt; this makes about 2 portions.

5. Apple hand pies

Cut one approximately 250 g sheet of ready-rolled pastry into 8 rectangles. Fill 4 with a cooled mixture of 1 finely diced apple, 1 tablespoon sugar, ½ teaspoon cinnamon and 1 teaspoon flour. Add the remaining rectangles, seal edges, vent tops and brush with beaten egg if desired. Bake at 200°C / 400°F for about 20–25 minutes until well risen and golden, checking the pastry pack instructions.

6. Layered apple yogurt cups

Cool a batch of the skillet apples completely. Layer between 250 g plain thick yogurt and 40 g granola in 2 glasses. Add the granola immediately before serving if you want crunch. This turns cooked fruit into a chilled dessert without making a second dough or batter.

7. Cinnamon apple toast

Toast 2 thick bread slices and spread with a little butter. Top with warm skillet apples, divided evenly, and finish with a spoonful of plain yogurt. Serve immediately so the toast stays crisp. Use a plate rather than a serving board to catch the fruit syrup.

8. Apple oat dessert cookies

Mix 80 g oats, 60 g flour, ½ teaspoon baking powder, ½ teaspoon cinnamon and a pinch of salt. Stir in 50 g melted cooled butter, 40 g brown sugar and 1 beaten egg, then fold in 80 g finely diced apple. Scoop 10 portions onto a lined tray and bake at 180°C / 350°F for roughly 14–18 minutes until the edges set and turn golden. Cool on the tray before moving; these are soft fruit cookies, not crisp biscuits.

Frequently asked questions

Which apples should I choose?

Use firm apples for pastry roses and halved baked apples. A mixture of sweet and tart apples works in crumble; adjust added sugar to taste.

Can I prepare the crumble ahead?

Keep prepared fruit and topping separately refrigerated, then assemble before baking. The topping can become damp when it sits directly on the fruit.

Can I skip the nuts?

Yes. In the baked apples, replace the walnuts with a little more oats if that suits your ingredients. Check all labels and handling requirements for guests with allergies.
