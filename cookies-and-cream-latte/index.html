Cookies and Cream Latte: An Iced Coffee Recipe

Make an iced cookies and cream latte with cocoa, vanilla, coffee and milk, then finish with fine cookie crumbs and an optional cream topping.

A cookies and cream latte works best when the coffee stays drinkable and the cookie crumbs remain a small topping. This iced version uses a cocoa-vanilla accent in the espresso, milk over ice and a spoonful of cream on top. It is an original recipe concept with adjustable sweetness.

Ingredients for one glass

Prepare 30–60 ml espresso or 60 ml strong brewed coffee, 180 ml milk, 1 teaspoon unsweetened cocoa powder, 1–2 teaspoons sugar, a few drops of vanilla extract, ice and one chocolate sandwich cookie. Optional topping: 2 tablespoons softly whipped cream.

Set aside the cookie until serving. Blending several cookies into the milk makes a thicker dessert drink, which is a different result. For this latte, the cocoa carries some cookie-like flavor without putting gritty crumbs throughout the glass.

Dissolve the cocoa in hot coffee

Mix the cocoa and sugar with a small splash of hot coffee to make a smooth paste. Stir in the rest of the coffee and the vanilla. This step helps prevent dry cocoa from floating in clumps when it meets cold milk.

Let the mixture cool a little before assembly. If using brewed coffee, make it concentrated enough to hold its flavor after adding milk and ice. Taste and adjust sugar modestly, remembering that the cookie topping contributes sweetness.

Assemble the iced latte

Fill a glass with ice, add the milk and pour in the cocoa-coffee mixture. Stir until the color is even. Spoon the optional soft cream on top rather than piping a large frosting-like layer.

Crush half the cookie into fine crumbs and scatter them over the top immediately before serving. Serve the other half alongside the glass. Avoid a thick layer of coarse crumbs that can fall into the drink and make the last sip gritty.

Adjust the flavor

For a stronger coffee version, use the upper end of the espresso amount and reduce the milk slightly. For a lighter dessert accent, use half a teaspoon of cocoa and keep the cookie only on the side. Decaf works with the same proportions.

An unsweetened plant-based milk can be used, but its flavor will change the drink. Check cookie and topping ingredients for relevant allergens. If skipping cream, add the fine crumbs just before drinking or leave the cookie on a separate plate.

Troubleshoot texture and timing

Cocoa clumps usually mean it was not dissolved in hot liquid first. A watery drink may need stronger coffee or less ice melt, not extra cookie crumbs. If the latte is too sweet, lower the sugar on the next serving.

Prepare one glass at a time and add the crumbs at the end. Keep milk and cream refrigerated until needed and follow their product guidance. The AI illustration is a serving reference; this drink is not claimed to replicate a named café recipe.

Frequently asked questions

Should I blend the cookie into the latte?

Not for this version. Keep fine crumbs as a topping so the coffee remains smoother.

Can I make it without espresso?

Yes. Use 60 ml concentrated brewed coffee and adjust milk to taste.

Is the cream necessary?

No. It is optional; serve the cookie beside the glass if you prefer a simpler topping.
