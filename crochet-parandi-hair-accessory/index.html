Crochet Parandi: A Braid Accessory Guide

Make a lightweight crochet tassel braid accent with simple caps, cotton cords and comfortable attachment tips inspired by Punjabi parandi styling.

By Folvella Editorial · Updated 2026-10-04 · 3 min read

Images are original AI-generated styling illustrations, not product or service photographs.

A parandi is a decorative braid accessory associated with Punjabi hair traditions. This guide makes a small crochet tassel accent inspired by that form; it is a contemporary beginner project rather than a traditional construction pattern. Keep the accessory light and attach it to a finished braid without pulling on your hair.

In this guide

  1. Choose a light material
  2. Crochet three simple tassel caps
  3. Add the tassels and cords
  4. Attach it to a finished braid
  5. Three color directions

Choose a light material

Use cotton embroidery thread or fine cotton yarn, a crochet hook matched to its label, scissors and a blunt yarn needle. You will also need three short cotton cords for the tassel tops. Choose burgundy and gold for a warm combination, or two shades of the same color for a quieter result.

Make a sample tassel before choosing the final size. Heavy beads or thick yarn can weigh down a braid. Aim for a soft accessory that swings freely without pulling the hair tie downward.

Crochet three simple tassel caps

In US crochet terminology, chain 4 and join the ends with a slip stitch to make a ring. Chain 1, work 8 single crochet stitches into the ring, and join to the first stitch. Work two more rounds of 8 single crochet stitches, without increasing, to form a small open cup. Finish and weave in the tail.

Make three matching cups. This is a small starting pattern, and yarn thickness will change the finished size. If the cup is too tight for your tassel bundle, repeat the sample using 10 stitches per round rather than stretching it over the yarn.

Add the tassels and cords

Cut about 12 strands of thread twice the desired tassel length. Fold the bundle over a cotton cord, then wrap a separate strand around the bundle just below the fold and tie securely. Pull the cord through the center of a crochet cap with your yarn needle so the cap sits over the wrapped neck.

Repeat for the other two tassels, then trim their ends evenly. Braid the three upper cords together into one short attachment loop. Tug each component gently before putting the accessory in your hair; loose threads should be secured now.

Attach it to a finished braid

Tie your hair into a comfortable braid first. Loop the accessory around the existing hair elastic and fasten it with a soft knot. Position the tassels below the end of the braid so they are visible without being woven tightly against your scalp.

For a longer look, lengthen the cotton cords rather than adding heavier tassels. Remove the accessory before sleep, exercise or any activity where it might catch. Start with a short wearing session to check comfort.

Three color directions

Try a single-color cotton version with matching cords for everyday wear. A two-color version can alternate tassel colors while keeping the caps uniform. For an occasion, use a small amount of metallic thread wrapped around the tassel neck; keep rough or stiff thread away from the hair.

When sharing the finished project, credit the parandi tradition and describe your version accurately as crochet-inspired. The original AI illustration shows a styling possibility, not the exact result of every yarn and hook combination.

Frequently asked questions

Is this a traditional parandi pattern?

No. It is a contemporary crochet tassel project inspired by the form of a Punjabi braid accessory.

Can a beginner make it?

The crochet uses chains, slip stitches and single crochet in US terminology. Practice one cap before making the full set.

How do I keep it comfortable?

Use light thread, avoid heavy beads and attach it to a finished braid elastic rather than pulling it into the roots.
