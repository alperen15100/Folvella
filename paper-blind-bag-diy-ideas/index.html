10 Paper Blind Bag Ideas to Make at Home

Make paper surprise bags with a simple folding method, ten handmade filling ideas and tips for keeping each little package easy to open.

A paper blind bag is a small handmade package whose contents stay hidden until it is opened. The fun can come from the wrapping and the thought inside, without buying a set of collectible toys. Keep the contents light so the paper keeps its shape.

This guide uses your own drawings and ordinary craft supplies. It does not include downloadable templates or licensed character artwork.

In this guide

  2. Ten contents you can make yourself

Fold one sample before making a batch

Use a sheet of ordinary paper, a ruler, glue stick and blunt-ended scissors where appropriate. Fold the two long sides toward the center with a small overlap; glue that overlap to form a tube. Leave it open until the seam is dry.

Fold a short section of the bottom upward, open that fold into a diamond and bring the two points together with overlap. Glue the bottom closed, let it set and gently open the bag. Test with one light item before changing the paper size.

Ten contents you can make yourself

1. Mini bookmark: cut a narrow card strip and draw a simple border. Leave the top slightly exposed inside tissue for a satisfying reveal.

2. Paper flower: make a flat daisy from two paper circles and petals. Keep it thin enough that it does not crush against the seam.

3. Tiny encouragement card: write a personal sentence on folded card. Avoid promising a mystery prize that the package cannot deliver.

4. Drawing prompt: include a small card with one original sketch idea. A matching colored pencil can stay outside the bag.

5. Origami heart: fold it from a square of thin paper. Test the finished shape in the sample bag before sealing.

6. Mini paper notebook: fold two sheets and place them inside a card cover. Keep it unstapled if a staple could scratch the recipient.

7. Handmade paper stickers: use your own designs on suitable sticker paper. Include backing so the adhesive stays protected.

8. Color palette card: paint or draw five small swatches. Let them dry fully before packing against another item.

9. Puzzle message: cut a simple note into a few large pieces. Write how many pieces belong in the packet.

10. Small photo keepsake: use a copy of a personal photo with a card backing. Ask permission before sharing another person’s image publicly.

Wrap, label and open

Place the filling inside loose tissue and fold the top twice. Use a small paper tab or removable sticker rather than covering the whole opening in tape. If several bags belong to a set, number them on the back and keep a private note of their contents.

For a child’s activity, choose contents suitable for that child’s age and supervise cutting and gluing. Avoid tiny loose embellishments for young children. Photograph the closed and opened version separately when sharing the project so the surprise stays clear.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need printed templates?

No. A paper tube with a folded base is enough for the basic bag.

What paper works best?

Test ordinary paper first; thicker paper may need firmer folds and a stronger compatible adhesive.

Can I sell these?

Use your own designs and accurately describe the contents, materials and age suitability.
