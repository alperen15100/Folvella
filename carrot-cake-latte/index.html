Carrot Cake Latte: A Warm Spiced Coffee Recipe

Make a carrot cake-inspired latte with cinnamon, ginger, nutmeg and vanilla, plus hot, iced and decaf options for a cozy coffee break.

By Folvella Editorial · Updated 2026-10-04 · 3 min read

Images are original AI-generated styling illustrations, not product or service photographs.

This carrot cake latte takes its direction from warm baking spices, vanilla and a small amount of brown sugar. It does not need raw carrot juice or cake blended into coffee. The result is a simple spiced latte with an optional cake accompaniment, made as an original home-kitchen recipe.

In this guide

  1. Measure a restrained spice mix
  2. Warm the milk gently
  3. Build the latte
  4. Make an iced or decaf version
  5. Keep the flavor balanced

Measure a restrained spice mix

For one mug, use 30–60 ml espresso or 60 ml strongly brewed coffee, 180 ml milk, 1–2 teaspoons brown sugar, ⅛ teaspoon ground cinnamon, a tiny pinch of ground ginger, a tiny pinch of nutmeg and a few drops of vanilla extract.

Use small pinches for ginger and nutmeg rather than equal spoonfuls of every spice. Too much dry spice can taste dusty and overwhelm the coffee. Optional topping: a spoonful of softly whipped cream and a light cinnamon dusting.

Warm the milk gently

Combine the milk, sugar and spices in a small saucepan. Warm over low heat, stirring until the sugar dissolves. Keep it below a boil and remove it once comfortably steaming. Add the vanilla after taking it off the heat.

The spices will not completely dissolve. For a smoother drink, strain the warmed milk through a fine tea strainer before serving. You can also use slightly less spice and add a tiny dusting only at the end.

Build the latte

Pour the coffee into a mug and add the warm spiced milk. Stir, taste and adjust sweetness with a small additional amount of sugar only if needed. If using a milk frother, froth a portion of the warmed milk separately and spoon it over the top.

Serve with a small piece of carrot cake if you want the full flavor association. The drink itself contains no carrot in this version. Its name describes a baking-spice direction, not a claim that it provides the nutritional content of vegetables.

Make an iced or decaf version

For an iced drink, let the sweetened spiced milk cool before pouring it over ice with the coffee. Strain first if you want to avoid settled spice. Use a slightly stronger coffee base because melting ice will soften the flavor.

Decaf espresso or concentrated decaf coffee works in either version. Unsweetened oat milk can give a rounded taste; choose a product that works with heating if making a hot latte. Different milks will change the texture, so adjust the frothing step to the product.

Keep the flavor balanced

If the drink tastes mostly like cinnamon, reduce the spice before adding more sugar. If the coffee disappears, use stronger coffee or less milk. If grit collects at the bottom, strain the infused milk and use finer or fewer spices next time.

Make the latte close to serving, and keep dairy refrigerated until you use it. The AI image is an autumn serving illustration, not proof of a tested recipe or a branded café match. Adjust the recipe to your own sweetness and coffee preferences.

Frequently asked questions

Does this latte contain carrot?

No. This version uses the spice direction of carrot cake and offers cake as an optional accompaniment.

Why are there spices at the bottom?

Ground spices do not dissolve completely. Use less spice or strain the warmed milk before assembling the drink.

Can I drink it iced?

Yes. Cool the spiced milk first, then add it to ice and coffee; strain if you want a smoother texture.
