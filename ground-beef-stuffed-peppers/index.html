7 Ground Beef Stuffed Pepper Ideas with a Reliable Base Recipe

Make ground beef stuffed peppers with cooked rice, tomato and seven flavor variations, plus practical baking and make-ahead steps.

Stuffed peppers turn a skillet filling into a complete baked dinner. A precooked beef-and-rice mixture gives you control over texture before it goes into the oven.

Start with the base recipe, then choose one of seven flavor combinations. The illustrations show serving ideas; the variations use one clear method rather than seven unrelated recipes.

A practical base recipe for four servings

Use 4 large bell peppers, 450 g ground beef (about 1 lb), 1 tablespoon olive oil, 1 small diced onion, 2 minced garlic cloves, 300 g cooked rice (about 1½ cups), 400 g canned chopped tomatoes, 1 teaspoon dried oregano and salt and pepper to taste. Optional topping: 60 g grated cheese.

Halve the peppers lengthwise and remove seeds and membranes. Using cooked rice keeps the filling predictable; uncooked rice needs a different liquid balance and method. Choose a baking dish that holds eight pepper halves upright without stacking them.

Cook the filling and bake the peppers

Heat the oven to 190°C/375°F. Place the pepper halves in the dish with about 60 ml water, cover with foil and bake for 15 minutes while making the filling. Heat the oil in a skillet, soften the onion, add garlic, then add the beef and break it into small pieces.

Cook the ground beef thoroughly, using a food thermometer to confirm 71°C/160°F. Add tomatoes and oregano and simmer until the filling is moist without being soupy, then fold in the cooked rice. Fill the pepper halves, cover and bake for about 20–25 minutes, until tender. Add optional cheese and uncover for a further 5–10 minutes.

1–3. Familiar stuffed pepper combinations

1. Tomato-and-oregano: use the base recipe and finish with chopped parsley. 2. Cheddar-and-pickle: replace oregano with a little mustard in the filling and add chopped pickles after baking. 3. Taco-inspired: use cumin and mild chili powder instead of oregano and serve with salsa.

Keep the filling quantity similar across versions. A generous mound should stay inside the pepper rather than spilling across the dish. If a sauce is salty, reduce the salt in the filling and taste before adding cheese or other concentrated toppings.

4–5. Grain and vegetable variations

4. Beef-and-quinoa: replace the cooked rice with a similar volume of cooked quinoa; the liquid needs remain manageable because the grain is already cooked. 5. Beef-and-mushroom: sauté 150 g finely chopped mushrooms until their released moisture evaporates, then add them with the beef.

For the mushroom version, reduce the rice slightly if the peppers are small. Do not add raw watery vegetables to the finished mixture and expect them to behave like a dry filling. Cook vegetables first so they contribute flavor without flooding the baking dish.

6–7. Two bolder finishes

6. Smoky paprika: add 1 teaspoon smoked paprika to the beef and finish with a little yogurt after baking. 7. Ginger-soy: replace oregano with grated ginger, use a small amount of soy sauce in place of some salt, and top the baked peppers with sliced scallions.

These are flavor variations on one base recipe, not claims about traditional regional dishes. Keep creamy sauces on the side for guests who prefer a different finish. Make-ahead and reheating notes

Prepare the cooked filling and peppers in advance, then refrigerate them promptly in suitable covered containers. For assembly, spoon the chilled filling into the peppers and allow extra oven time; check the center rather than relying on a clock alone.

Refrigerate leftovers within two hours, or within one hour when the surrounding temperature is above 32°C/90°F. Reheat leftovers to 74°C/165°F. Serve with a simple salad or bread, and store creamy toppings separately so they keep their texture.

Frequently asked questions

Should the rice be cooked first?

Yes for this recipe. Using uncooked rice changes the needed liquid and timing.

Can I replace beef with turkey?

Yes, but ground turkey must reach 74°C/165°F and may need a little additional oil or moisture.

Are these peppers high in protein?

Beef contributes protein, but the exact amount depends on the meat, grain, cheese and portion size. No calculated nutrition claim is provided.
