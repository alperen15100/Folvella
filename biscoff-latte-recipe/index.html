Biscoff Latte Recipe: Creamy Hot and Iced Cookie-Butter Coffee

Make a hot or iced Biscoff latte with smooth cookie butter, espresso and milk, plus easy swaps and tips to avoid clumps.

Cookie butter gives coffee a caramelized biscuit flavor without needing several different syrups. The important step is dissolving the spread in hot coffee before it meets the milk.

The hot and iced methods below use the same compact ingredient list. Choose your toppings for the occasion and keep the drink's sweetness adjustable.

What you need for one cookie-butter latte

Use 1 tablespoon smooth Biscoff spread or another smooth cookie butter, 30–60 ml espresso, and 180 ml milk. Add ice for the chilled version. Optional finishing ingredients are a spoonful of whipped cream and half a crushed caramelized biscuit; the drink works without either.

Cookie butter is already sweet, so begin without added syrup or sugar. Check the spread and biscuit labels for wheat, soy, milk or other allergens relevant to your guests. Different brands vary in sweetness and spice, and a gluten-free result requires suitable ingredients throughout.

Dissolve the spread before adding milk

Place the cookie butter in a heat-safe cup and pour the hot espresso over it. Stir until the mixture is smooth. If thick streaks remain, warm the mixture briefly and keep stirring; avoid overheating or boiling it.

Using this hot-coffee step helps the spread disperse before it meets cold milk. Dropping a spoonful into an iced glass tends to leave waxy clumps at the bottom. Crunchy cookie butter also leaves biscuit particles, so smooth spread is the more predictable option for this drink.

Make an iced Biscoff latte

Fill a glass with ice, add the milk and pour in the smooth espresso-and-cookie-butter mixture. Stir thoroughly. For a stronger coffee flavor, use a double espresso with the same amount of milk rather than adding more spread.

Whipped cream and a few crumbs create a dessert-style finish. Add them just before serving so the crumbs stay crisp. Keep decoration off the outer rim if it makes the glass sticky or difficult to hold; a clean serving glass is more useful than a heavy coating of spread.

Make a hot cookie-butter latte

Warm the milk gently in a saucepan or with your usual milk frother, following the appliance instructions. Pour it into the dissolved cookie-butter espresso and stir. Leave space at the top if you plan to add foam or whipped cream.

For an extra-spiced version, add a tiny pinch of cinnamon to the hot coffee mixture. For a lighter dessert impression, use half a tablespoon of spread and skip the toppings. The drink is adjustable, but it should not be described as low-sugar or low-calorie without calculating the actual ingredients.

Make it without an espresso machine

Use 60 ml strongly brewed coffee from a moka pot, French press or another familiar method. Instant coffee also works: dissolve it in a small amount of hot water according to the package instructions, then mix in the spread before adding milk.

For guests, prepare individual drinks rather than leaving a large dairy-based batch at room temperature. The spread can settle as the drink cools, so stir before serving. If you choose a plant-based milk, select an unsweetened version first and adjust only after tasting.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use crunchy cookie butter?

You can, but biscuit pieces may remain in the drink. Smooth spread is easier to dissolve.

Do I need extra sugar?

Usually not to start. Taste the finished drink because cookie butter is already sweet.

Can I make it decaf?

Yes. Replace the espresso with decaf espresso or a small amount of strong decaf coffee.
